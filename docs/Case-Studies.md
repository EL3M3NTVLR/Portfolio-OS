## Legaloid
**Design Thinking and Innovation, Christ University, 2026 (group project)**

**Overview.** A guided legal-assistance platform for first-time legal help seekers in India: verified lawyer discovery, plain-language guidance and case tracking in one journey.

**The question.** I started from watching someone close to me struggle through a first encounter with the legal system with no guidance and nobody to ask. It made me wonder why there is no platform that walks first-timers through the process. (check: this version leaves out the personal details on purpose; please keep it this way.)

**My role.** I came up with the original idea and pitch, fleshed out the solution, did the market research and designed the prototype. The team (Aritra Banerjee, Ansh Banerjee, Hrishika Jain, Tavishi Batra) developed it into a presentation and research article.

**How it came together.**
- I assumed cost was the main barrier, then tested it with a survey. Of ten young adults, seven had first-time legal experience. Only two named legal fees as a top challenge, while all seven struggled to understand legal procedures, and stress averaged 4.0 out of 5. We dropped the cost-led idea.
- The prototype became one six-step journey (describe your issue, find a verified advocate, know before booking, book and upload documents, track your case). Layouts were generated in Google Stitch and refined in Figma.
- A box to describe what happened replaced the legal-category picker, because many people do not know how to classify their own problem.

**Outcome.** In unassisted testing with six first-time or near-first-time legal seekers, five completed all four tasks without help, and self-reported stress fell from 4.0 to 2.3 out of 5. Faculty gave strong feedback and encouraged us to take it forward. Source note: team survey and usability test, small samples; they show direction, not proof.

**Reflection.** Starting from what people experience led us to reject our own assumption before building on it. A cost-led product would have been well made and poorly aimed.

**Links.** Presentation, prototype, research article.

## HerbTantra (App Prototype)
**Personal project, before the internship**

**Overview.** An app prototype for Herb Tantra, an Ayurvedic wellness brand, built before I had been hired by them.

**The question.** I was just learning Figma and UI/UX principles and wanted to apply to Herb Tantra as an intern. So I made something for them instead of only sending a résumé.

**My role.** Everything: I researched the brand, designed the Figma prototype and put together the presentation.

**How it came together.** I researched the brand first so the prototype would feel on-brand, then designed the Figma prototype and a presentation to show it.

**Outcome.** The founder appreciated the initiative and how on-brand the prototype was, and asked how viable the app would be and how it would benefit him beyond the existing website. The prototype helped me land the internship (Apr to May 2025).

**Reflection.** A made thing speaks louder than an application.

**Link.** Figma prototype (see projects.json).

## Heartware (Documentary)
**College assignment, group project**

**Overview.** A documentary exploring dating apps and the culture of modern dating.

**The question.** The assignment asked for a slightly explorative, research-based documentary. As a group we chose dating apps and dating culture.

**My role.** I shot and edited the film. My teammates appeared on screen, conducted interviews, set up shots, handled art direction and put together the background research.

**How it came together.** We interviewed many strangers as well as ourselves, so the film carries both outside and insider perspectives. The camera had no built-in stabilization and we could not source a gimbal, which limited moving shots, so we shaped some concepts and techniques so that camera shake would read as part of the picture.

**Outcome.** One of my favourite pieces so far, and it earned the group a great grade.

**Reflection.** Dating can be superficial, but people differ widely in how stereotypes are perceived and preferred.

## Mirage (Music Video)
**SDG 6: Clean Water and Sanitation, Sep 2026, a Group 1 production**

**Overview.** A music video built around water shortage.

**The question.** We were tasked with making a music video on any Sustainable Development Goal. I chose water shortage because I experienced it firsthand in Bengaluru in 2024.

**My role.** I ideated and directed it, sang in it and starred in it.

**How it came together.** Directing while performing worked because of my team, especially the DOP and the AD: I described my vision to them, then went out and performed without worrying about the shot. Making it also let me dabble in music production.

**Credits.** Directed by Aritra Banerjee; Director of Photography Vaibhavi Chaturvedi; Assistant Director and Editor Aditya Deo Prasad; Screenplay Vaibhavi Chaturvedi; Music Producer Swapnendu Mukherjee; Vocals Swapnendu Mukherjee, Aritra Banerjee, Evan Joshua Joseph; Line Producer and BTS Co-ordinator Ansh Banerjee; Starring Aritra Banerjee.

**Outcome.** Great feedback from faculty, and it counted heavily towards our grade.

**Reflection.** I would give myself more time to be thorough and use better equipment to be more artistically enabled.

## Museum of a Normal Person (Documentary Series)
**3 episodes, college assignment**

**Overview.** A 3-part mini-docu series about the craft of sourcing, curating and restoring trinkets, antiques and furniture, with an academic angle.

**The question.** The assignment was a "Humans of Bangalore" style docu-series. We picked Mr Ramachandran and his store, found through Instagram, because it was a fascinating place made of ordinary things.

**My role.** Cinematographer, editor and colorist (check: your résumé says cinematographer and colorist only; add "editor" if you want them to match). Labour was divided fairly equally across the team.

**How it came together.** We graded the series earthy and warm to reflect the groundedness of the stories behind each item. The academic angle asks what even counts as curation, and what the provenance of everyday antiques is.

**Outcome.** It contributed a lot to our grade, drew good faculty feedback, and brought me closer to the people and stories behind every piece in the store.

**Reflection.** I want to travel and meet many more people so every piece I make carries those experiences and resonates with viewers.
